## The one-sentence brief

Today is the **final lesson — graduation day.** Three acts. **Act 1 · The final leg** — sprint discipline in four words: **fix-only, no adding**; the last review upgrades into **the release check** (can it face people?), anchored by **the engineer's checklist** (Brandy's four universal checks + a fallback plan). **Act 2 · Tell the world** — ten lessons of logs merge into **a README** (AI organizes, the student proofreads — *the catch*), the **cost & value math** (two lines only: what it cost, what it's worth — no profits, no business talk), a **one-page pitch** (four boxes held up by **the three hard references**), and a **rehearsal with an AI investor** ("So what? What's it to me?"). **Act 3 · The showcase & graduation** — the **live roadshow** (demo first, then talk; the 2-minute hourglass exempts nobody; every desk rotates, one per desk to the stage), **the vote** (3 votes for "the one you'd most want to see really exist" — not yourself), and the **closing ceremony** — the **ten-lesson journey, all lit**, the bookend back to Lesson 1, the last log, and the photo.

**The whole lesson runs in three acts** (the rhythm skeleton — keep this map in your head all day):

| Act | Blocks | Total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Act 1 · The final leg** | Opening (the final-day map) → leg-three stand-up → the sprint (fix-only) → the release check | 70 min |
| **Act 2 · Tell the world** | README (the logs merge) → the cost & value math → the one-page pitch → the AI investor rehearsal | 57 min |
| **Act 3 · The showcase & graduation** | The live roadshow → the vote → the closing ceremony | 53 min |

Each act boundary has one explicit **gear-change move**: Act 1→2 is "hands off — what passes the release check earns the right to be told"; Act 2→3 is "pitch closed — real humans on stage."

**When they leave, students can** (three things):

1. Turn ten lessons of logs into **one README** — AI organizes, the student proofreads for inventions ("did it invent anything?");
2. Say **who their project is for, and what it's worth** — cost + value, and stop there (the middle-school line: no business taught);
3. Deliver **one 2-minute roadshow: demo first** (the thing really moves), then the pitch.

**Not yet, don't worry** (three things):

1. Business models / profit margins — the middle-school red line: not discussed; we stop at "cost + what I think it's worth";
2. A perfect project — the roadshow scores "told clearly + it really moves"; confessing flaws is a *plus*;
3. Not being nervous — nerves are normal; the AI rehearsal exists precisely to burn up "being questioned" before the stage.

**When things go wrong:** swap (hardware) → cut to backup (tool / account / network) → ask AI (knowledge).

**Today's three iron laws:**
1. **The closing ceremony is 15 minutes, and no situation cuts it** — 15 minutes before the end, whatever you're doing, the ceremony begins (roadshow remainder: present after the ceremony or post video to the class group);
2. **Fix-only** — every new feature goes to the "later" list;
3. **Recording rules** — hands only, never faces, the whole day; when parents are present, recording stays strictly inside the consent list.

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

*By the end of this session, students will be able to…*

1. **Create** a README from their ten lesson logs and **verify** it against the source logs — catching anything AI invented (*Bloom: Create / Evaluate*).
2. **Explain** their project in cost-and-value terms — what it cost, what it's worth and why — without sliding into business talk (*Bloom: Explain*).
3. **Perform** a 2-minute roadshow: demonstrate the working project first, then deliver the one-page pitch built on the three hard references (*Bloom: Apply / Create*).

## Page One — read this before class starts

### What today produces (four student outputs)

1. **One README** — the ten logs merged into a self-introduction + growth story, proofread by the student (AI inventions caught and deleted);
2. **One two-line account** — what it cost / what it's worth and why (one number + one reason);
3. **One one-page pitch** — four boxes (problem / solution / who it's for / what it's worth), held up by the three hard references;
4. **One 2-minute roadshow** — demo first, then pitch; every student presents (100% roadshow rate is the acceptance line).

### Three things you need to do

1. **Follow the clock — and the two hard gates.** Work down the timeline; the gates are non-negotiable: **stop the sprint when Act 1 ends; enter the ceremony 15 minutes before the end.** Whatever's running, you shift.
2. **Read the lines.** Every 🗣️ line is pre-written classroom speech — read it as-is, don't improvise. The ceremony script (6.8) deserves two rehearsals — it's the full stop on ten lessons.
3. **Guard the recording rules.** Hands only, never faces; the guardian-consent list decides who may be on camera; unconsented students get back-of-head / hands / project close-ups.

### Three things you do NOT need to do

1. **You don't build anything today.** Fix-only means students fix; you circulate (one lap per 15 minutes, the three looks).
2. **You don't polish their README or pitch.** AI organizes, the student proofreads — you only spot-check: "Did you write this in any log?"
3. **You don't teach business.** Cost is applied math; value is an expression exercise; profits and business models stay out. One line if challenged: "We're teaching how to explain a creation — not how to run a business."

### When things go wrong

- **A project dies on stage** → the standard plan: play the log's demo photo/video + present live — and turn it into the teaching moment: "See? That's engineer's demo-backup awareness."
- **A student freezes** → the buffer already exists (desk-level first); still frozen → the pair-up (one demos, one talks); the floor is *never absent*.
- **No network** → all AI blocks cut: the math by hand on paper, the pitch four boxes handwritten, the rehearsal becomes desk-mate Q&A; **the release check, the roadshow, and the ceremony run in every situation.**
- **You're stumped:** smile, and say *"Let's ask AI together."*
- One-liner: **swap, cut to backup, ask AI.**

## Section 3 · Segment-by-segment script

### Segment 1 ｜ Opening + the final-day map (5 min) 【Act 1 · The final leg】

🗣️ **Say this:**

> "30-second recap: last session you called in reinforcements — code help and look help. Your project's function capped out, and it had a face for the first time. Baton handoff sheet? On the desk — we read it at the stand-up."
>
> "Today — **the last lesson. Graduation day.** Three parts *(projected)*: the final leg, fix-only → tell the world (README, the math, the pitch, the roadshow) → the closing ceremony. Today has exactly one job: **make your project understood.**"

👀 **Students do:** baton sheet on the desk; listen to the final-day map.

⚠️ **Pitfalls:**
- "Is there an exam?" → "No exam. Today's 'judges' are real listeners — people who understand you are the ones your project deserves."
- A student missed Lesson 9 (no leg-two baton sheet) → TA A spends 5 minutes building a mini sheet (what it can demo now + what to fix today); they join the rotation normally.

📌 **Output anchor:** baton sheet on the desk; the three-part map seen.

---

### Segment 2 ｜ Leg-three stand-up (5 min) 【Act 1 · The final leg】

**Two ways to run it, as the first two legs** (small room: everyone stands and says one line; big room: pairs + write at the top of the requirements sheet + call 3–4).

🗣️ **Say this:**

> "Stand-up. First read your baton sheet — what did last leg say is 'the first thing next leg'? Today, one sentence: **'I'm going to fix ___, so the demo holds steady at ___.'** Remember the discipline *(point at the big type)*: **fix-only.** The new feature you're itching to add? The 'later' list welcomes it."

👀 **Students do:** read the baton sheet; one-sentence goal.

⚠️ **Pitfalls:**
- The goal contains "add a ___" → stop it on the spot: "That's an *add*. Make it a *fix* — which part are you least confident about? Fix that."
- Baton sheet lost → it's on the Brief Wall — re-read it on the spot and copy it back.

📌 **Output anchor:** one fix-only goal per student, verifiable.

---

### Segment 3 ｜ The sprint: fix-only (45 min) 【Act 1 · The final leg】

🗣️ **Say this:**

> "Go. Two jobs only. One: fix the faults the review left behind. Two: **polish demo stability** — on roadshow day it must work first try, in front of strangers. **Five clean demos in a row, that's 'steady.'**"
>
> "No shell yet? Don't start one today — if the Lesson-9 four moves can close it in 10 minutes, close it; otherwise it goes on stage as is. **Function is the required question.**"
>
> "When Act 1 ends, everyone stops — into the release check."

**Your circulation rhythm:** same as the first two legs — one lap per 15 minutes, three looks (screen / requirements sheet / face); new features → point at the projected discipline; unstable demos → guide "the five-clean-demos test."

⚠️ **Pitfalls:**
- A project crashes at this late stage → the final-leg crash line: "Don't fix it — **tell an honest failure. It's a full-score roadshow too**: where you got stuck, what you tried, what you learned. Often it's the best pitch in the room."
- Fast finisher, five clean already → be the neighbor's barbarian tester; or polish README material (flip through your logs).

📌 **Output anchor:** review leftovers fixed; demo stable (five clean) or the honest-failure route chosen.

---

### Segment 4 ｜ The release check (15 min — the review floor) 【Act 1 · The final leg】

🗣️ **Say this:**

> "Hands off. The last review — and the persona upgrades: **the release check.** Not fault-picking: 'can it face people?' Have AI open the final checklist *(project prompt 6.1)*; this time you go through it line by line, and every line gets a conclusion."
>
> "Checklist in hand, tick each item. **The 'how do I save it if it fails' line must have an answer** — no answer? Use the backup: demo photo/video from your logs."
>
> *(switch to slide 6, the engineer's checklist)* "Now the professional version — Brandy ran these four before sharing her voice keyboard: does the light come on immediately? does the recording end properly? is the recognized text complete? did the prompt land in the right window? The first two ask 'is it actually working?'; the last two ask 'is the work complete?'. **Map them onto your project's equivalents and copy them into your checklist.** She also kept a fallback plan: serial logs, screen recording, a finished video — your 'how do I save it' is exactly that."

👀 **Students do:** generate the release checklist → tick every item → confirm the backup plan.

⚠️ **Pitfalls:**
- "It's fine, no need to check." → "The engineer's superstition: the thing you don't check is the thing that breaks. Ten minutes."
- A real problem found, can't finish fixing it → into the README/pitch under 'not perfect yet' — confessing flaws is a plus (the principle since Lesson 8).

📌 **Output anchor:** a release checklist with every item concluded + a confirmed "how do I save it."

---

### Segment 5 ｜ README: ten logs become one (20 min) 【Act 2 · Tell the world】

🗣️ **Say this** *(README, first mention):*

> "Ten lessons, ten logs. Today they merge into **a README** — your project's 'self-introduction + growth story': someone who never sees the project, reading only this, understands what it is and how it got here."
>
> "Same old rule: AI organizes, **you proofread.** Prompt *(project 6.2)* — and watch the last line: **it's not allowed to invent.**"
>
> *(after generation)* "Line by line: **did it invent anything?** *(project the teacher's rehearsal 'catch' — AI added a feature that's in no log.)* Caught one? Delete it — **AI will confidently invent; proofreading is your job.** Ten lessons in, this is the final open-book exam."

👀 **Students do:** feed the logs → generate → proofread and catch → copy the skeleton into the notebook (template projected, 6.2).

⚠️ **Pitfalls:**
- Too few logs (<5) → add 2 spoken recollections first ("take a photo, talk for 30 seconds"), then generate.
- Student accepts everything unproofread → TA spot-check: "Did you write this in any log?"

📌 **Output anchor:** a README in the student document, proofread, inventions caught.

---

### Segment 6 ｜ The cost & value math (12 min) 【Act 2 · Tell the world】

🗣️ **Say this** *(the lite line — the red line goes up first):*

> "Rule first: **today we count exactly two things — what it cost, and what it's worth. No profits, no business talk.** Counting cost is applied math; saying value is an expression exercise."
>
> "Step one: list your materials — every component; have AI check for gaps (shell materials count too, even a takeout box). Step two: **if you sold it, what would you price it at? And why?** Write one number, write one reason. Note: the price can be above cost — even well above — **but the reason must be 'it solves this problem,' not 'I want it.'**"

👀 **Students do:** list materials → AI cross-check → write "I think it's worth ___, because ___."

⚠️ **Pitfalls:**
- "Ten thousand!" → catch it: "Fine — now make the 'why' hold up. Can't? Lower the price until you can." (This is the core move of value-expression training — don't scold.)
- A parent in the room challenges "teaching kids about money" → the locked line: "We're teaching how to explain a creation — cost is applied math, value is expression training. We're not teaching business."

📌 **Output anchor:** one number + one reason per student.

---

### Segment 7 ｜ The one-page pitch (15 min) 【Act 2 · Tell the world】

🗣️ **Say this** *(pitch, first mention):*

> "A pitch — one page — **the page that makes someone understand and care within 2 minutes.** Four boxes: problem, solution, who it's for, what it's worth *(project template 6.4)*. AI helps with structure and wording — but three **hard references** it can't fake:"
>
> "**'Who it's for' must be a real person from your Lesson-2 list; 'what it's worth' must quote the account you just did; 'the problem' must be your requirements sheet's own words.** All three real, and the page has bones."

👀 **Students do:** AI-collaborate the four boxes → check against the three hard references → finalize (into the notebook).

⚠️ **Pitfalls:**
- A box invents a user or inflates the effect → send it back: "Three hard references — point each one out to me."

📌 **Output anchor:** a one-page pitch with all three hard references verified.

---

### Segment 8 ｜ The AI investor rehearsal (10 min) 【Act 2 · Tell the world】

🗣️ **Say this:**

> "The last gate before the stage: **an investor who only understands plain talk.** Its persona: **doesn't know tech, interrupts, and only asks 'So what? What's it to me?'** — read your pitch to it *(project prompt 6.5)*; get stumped, revise, read again. **Getting stumped doesn't lose points — this is where you spend your fear, so the stage has nothing left to take.**"

👀 **Students do:** one rehearsal round → revise the pitch once.

⚠️ **Pitfalls:**
- AI won't stay in persona (starts praising) → the old push-back: "No praise. Only 'so what?' questions."
- Stumped three times, deflated → "Every 'I can't answer' is a gift — turn it into one line of your pitch, and nobody on stage can stump you."

📌 **Output anchor:** a pitch that survived one investor round.

---

### Segment 9 ｜ The live roadshow (32 min) 【Act 3 · The showcase & graduation】

*(With parents invited: they enter before this segment opens, seated at the back; TA C guides them with one spoken line: "Today your child is showing you the complete work of ten lessons.")*

🗣️ **Say this:**

> "Three rules *(projected)*: **demo first, then talk** — the thing really moving says more than anything; **2 minutes each** — the hourglass exempts nobody (getting cut off is also the lesson: can't say it in 2 minutes = not clear yet); **every desk rotates** — and each desk sends one person to the stage."
>
> "The audience has a job too: 3 votes each, cast after hearing everyone, for the project **you'd most want to see really exist** — you can't vote for yourself."

👀 **Students do:** desk rotation (2 min each) → one per desk to the stage → listen, note your votes.

⚠️ **Pitfalls:**
- ⭐ **A project dies on stage** → the standard plan: play the log's demo photo/video + present live — and turn it into the teaching moment on the spot: "See? That's engineer's demo-backup awareness."
- ⭐ **A student freezes** → the buffer already exists (desk-level first); still frozen → the pair-up (one demos, one talks); the floor: never absent, never a blank slot.
- Over the hourglass, won't leave the stage → TA B raises the sign; finish this sentence, then wrap — discipline and dignity together.

📌 **Output anchor:** every student roadshows at desk level (100%); one per desk on stage.

---

### Segment 10 ｜ The vote (6 min) 【Act 3 · The showcase & graduation】

Voting (bottle caps / sticky notes / show of hands — no props bought) → TA C collects → the teacher comments on each one; **zero-vote projects get a comment naming one specific highlight — nobody walks away empty.**

📌 **Output anchor:** every project named once, including the overlooked.

---

### Segment 11 ｜ The closing ceremony (15 min — iron law) 【Act 3 · The showcase & graduation】

🗣️ **Say this** *(full script at 6.8; the ritual carries weight — pause one minute):*

> *(project the ten-lesson journey, point at it line by line)* "Lesson one, you lit your first lamp; two, you found a question worth doing; three, your project grew a screen; four, you got an AI team; five, you taught hardware to see; six, you moved your project home; seven, you briefed it; eight, you ran the first lap; nine, you called in reinforcements; **today — you told its story.** Ten lessons, all lit. You're the people who walked the whole way."
>
> "Remember Lesson 1? You lit your first piece of hardware, your name was on the screen, and you said 'whoa.' Ten lessons later — your name is on your project, in the README, in the applause just now. **You're not the one watching the magic anymore — you're the one making it.**"
>
> "The last log, four lines as usual. The 'did' line reads: **the proudest thing I made in ten lessons is ___.**"
>
> *(the photo)* "Photo — hold up your project, hands up, backs to the camera; old rule, hands only, never faces. **That's a wrap!**"

👀 **Students do:** write the last log; the photo; pack up the project + README to take home.

⚠️ **Pitfalls:**
- The ceremony gets squeezed by earlier blocks → **iron law: 15 minutes before the end, the ceremony begins** (roadshow remainder: present after the ceremony or post video to the class group). A teacher who squeezes the ceremony = a major backfill event.
- A student tears up / is moved → normal; it's part of the ritual's weight; your own sincerity is the only technique this block needs.

📌 **Output anchor:** the ten-lesson journey all lit; the last log written; the photo taken.

## Section 6 · Prompt phrase library (teacher reference)

> All of today's prompts and templates are maintained here — every reference in the segments matches this section word for word; the student document carries the same text. Project the whole block so students can copy it straight into AI. **Discipline restated: every AI round runs in the original project conversation — it knows the context of all ten logs.**

### 6.1 The release-check prompt (Segment 4)

```text
Based on the current state: my project can ___ now, and can't ___ yet.
You're a release inspector: in one hour it will demo in front of strangers.
Give me a "final pre-release checklist": every item to confirm before the
demo (enough power? cables seated? what's the first demo step?
how do I save it if it fails?).
```

### 6.2 The README prompt + copy-out template (Segment 5)

```text
These are my ten lesson logs: (paste all logs)
Using only these logs, organize them into a README with this template:
what it's called / whose problem it solves / how to use it (3 steps max) /
how it grew (pick 3 turning points) / what's not perfect yet /
what I'd still add.
Do not add anything I didn't write.
```

```text
# My Project README
## What it's called:
## Whose problem it solves (a real person's name):
## How to use it (3 steps max):
## How it grew (3 turning points: the brief / the stuck / the fix):
## What's not perfect yet (honestly, 1–2 lines):
## What I'd still add (pick from the "later" list):
```

### 6.3 The two-line account (Segment 6 — projected)

```text
Count exactly two things:
One. What it cost — the materials list (shell materials count, even a takeout box).
Two. What it's worth — I think it's worth ___, because ___.
(A high price is fine — but the reason must be "it solves this problem,"
not "I want it.")
```

### 6.4 The one-page pitch template (Segment 7)

```text
# My One-Page Pitch

## The problem (who is stuck with what — your requirements sheet's own words):

## The solution (how my project fixes it + the demo move):

## Who it's for (a real person from my Lesson-2 list):

## What it's worth (quoting my account: it cost ___, I think it's worth ___, because ___)
```

### 6.5 The AI investor rehearsal prompt (Segment 8)

```text
You are an investor who understands nothing about technology.
I'll read you my one-page pitch. You may interrupt me anytime,
but you may only ask two kinds of questions: "So what?" and
"What's it to me?" If you don't understand my answer,
keep pressing until you do.
```

> Push-back prompt when AI praises (projected): **"No praise. Only 'so what?' questions."**

### 6.6 Roadshow rules & the vote (Segments 9–10 — projected)

```text
The three roadshow rules:
One. Demo first, then talk — the thing really moving says more than anything.
Two. 2 minutes each — the hourglass exempts nobody.
Three. Every desk rotates; each desk sends one person to the stage.

The audience's job: 3 votes for the project you'd most want to see really
exist — you can't vote for yourself.
```

### 6.7 The roadshow self-check (before Segment 9 — projected + student document)

```text
☐ Demo first, then talk: I can say my first demo step from memory
☐ Five clean demos in a row
☐ Fits in 2 minutes (timed once)
☐ Four pitch boxes, none longer than two sentences
☐ "Who it's for" is a real person; "what it's worth" quotes my account
☐ Survived one round of "so what?" questions
☐ Backup in place: photo/video if the project dies
☐ I have an answer for "how do I save it if it fails"
```

### 6.8 The closing-ceremony script + the last log (Segment 11)

The ten-lesson journey (projected, read line by line): lit the first lamp → found a question worth doing → the project grew a screen → got an AI team → taught hardware to see → moved it home → briefed it → ran the first lap → called in reinforcements → **today, told its story.**

The last log's "did" line (projected): **The proudest thing I made in ten lessons is ___.**

The photo rule (projected): hold up your project, hands up, backs to the camera — **hands only, never faces.**

## Appendix · The partner/TTT-scene lines (this appendix is for partner sessions only)

> The master risk #8 requires two scene versions. The middle-school text above is locked to the lite line (cost + value, and stop). Partner/TTT sessions swap two spots:
