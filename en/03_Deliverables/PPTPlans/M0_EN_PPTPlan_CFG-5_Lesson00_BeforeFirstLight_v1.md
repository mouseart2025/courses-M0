## Design Principles

- **This deck has one job**: send every student into Lesson 1 carrying their **own question** — "the small thing in my life that annoys me is ___." Every page either builds trust (2–4), explains the game (5–10), or plants the seed (12).
- **Two center-of-gravity pages**: **slide 3** (your build story — told as a 90-second story, not read as specs) and **slide 12** (slow pace, one question, then silence). Everything else is support.
- **Pacing**: slides 1–2 fast; 3–4 slow and personal; 5–8 steady walk-through (the instructor must do slide 8 off script); 9–10 light sketch; 11 quick; **12 slowest of the deck**; 13 fast close.
- **Zero print**: nothing is handed out; every number, rule and question lives on screen.
- **One house rule is announced once, on slide 6** — and it is the only "rule" of the deck: *no hand-written code, ever*.

### Colors / Type / Icons / Layout

Follow the Chaihuo brand spec (`chaihuo-ppt-brand.md`): white 70 / yellow F3D230 15 / black 10 / red D84144 5; no gradients, no shadows, no complex illustrations; icons are 2 px black-stroke line icons; cards are white with a 1 px black border; emphasis boxes use a light-yellow fill or a yellow left border; numbered lists use yellow dot markers. **Red appears exactly once in this deck** — on slide 6 (the house rule), to mark it as the one non-negotiable. Script/quote boxes use the one permitted fifth color: light grey `#F5F5F5` fill + 1 px black border + monospace 14–16 pt.

### On-Screen Text Rules

- All on-screen text is final English copy (below, verbatim per slide).
- Numbers always shown as digits (`0 lines of hand-written code`, not "zero lines").
- Placeholders that the instructor must fill before class are marked `[FILL: …]` — slides 2 and 3 are the only hard-prep pages of the whole deck.

### Slide 06 | Your Role: Director & Programmer — Teaching point (house rule)

**Type**: Teaching point · **Time**: 5 min · **The only red page**

**On screen** (verbatim):

```text
Your Role — you're the director, AI is the programmer

YOU (the director)
· What to build — you set the brief
· What "good" means — you set the standard
· Whether it passes — you accept the work

AI (the programmer)
· Writes the code, builds the structure
· Fixes the bugs, runs the tests
· Always on call, never gets tired

House rule — 🔴
You do not write code by hand.
Your job is three things: say it clearly, watch what happens, tell it to change.
```

**Instructor notes**: announce the house rule with a light tone, frame it as good news. **We Do** — point at the screen and read the three things together, once: *"Say it clearly — watch what happens — tell it to change."* **CFU:** *"Thumbs up if your job is writing code. … Good — you're all wrong. Thumbs up if your job is saying it clearly, watching what happens, telling it to change."* ⚠️ **Parent/open-house note** (add one line here for adult audiences): *"If you're wondering 'but aren't they supposed to learn to code?' — code can be learned any time. What we build first is the ability to *direct* code — the part you can't catch up on later."*

### Slide 12 | Start Thinking Now — Call-to-action (the question)

**Type**: Call-to-action · **Time**: 4 min · **Memory point — never cut**

**On screen** (verbatim):

```text
Your Project Starts With a Question

What's one small thing in your daily life that annoys you?

At home, at school, on the way —
something that needs running after, something that needs watching.

For example:
the classroom light nobody turns off ｜ the pills grandma keeps forgetting ｜ plants that never get watered

You don't need to answer now. Remember it —
in Lesson 2, we turn it into your project.
```

**Instructor notes** — the memory point of the whole lesson. Slow your pace. Read the three examples, then **stay silent for 30 seconds and let them actually think**:

> **[Wait time: 30 s — do not fill the silence. Let it sit.]**

Land it: *"You don't need to answer now. Just remember it — better yet, jot it down in your phone notes tonight. In Lesson 2, we turn it into your project."* Someone blurts an answer mid-silence? Smile, take one sentence, say *"Remember it — we'll use it in Lesson 2"*, and keep the silence going. **TA note**: watch who is actually thinking (2–3 names — you can call on them at the start of Lesson 2).
