## Design Principles

- **A method session means sentence pages, not picture pages**: five-round openers, the compare page and the role cards are all projected in large type — students look at the screen and speak from it. Graphic skeleton pages appear exactly twice: the Five Roles map (slide 06) and the 8-step map (slide 19).
- **The "why" is taught, never staged**: slide 03's compare page is **static** — the instructor explains *why* plan-first beats write-it-direct; there is no live AI crash demo (today's AI usually gets it on the first pass — the plan never bets on a staged failure).
- **The handoff chain is the spine**: every relay round opens with *"based on the previous round's conclusion"* — handing the previous conversation's result to the new role. The baton lives in the conversation, not in the window count: all five rounds run in ONE conversation; switching roles = calling the name (John → Sally → Winston → Amelia → Quinn), never opening a new window.
- **The differentiator is the success line**: slide 07 sets the rule — "one thing must be different, and it must grow out of your life." Anti-sameness is graded: 20 identical timers = failure. The TA's #1 circulation focus is pushing vague differentiators ("mine is blue") to behavior: *"The moment you space out — what should the clock do?"*
- **State circles are the heart of the architect round**: slide 11 carries the only whole-class lecture (8 minutes) — the traffic-light analogy ("one light at a time, it changes when time's up"), then students draw their own state circles (at least WORK → BREAK, arrow labeled with what triggers the switch). Can't draw → copy the two-state starter → still stuck → skip architecture, go to the dev round with the two-state default (picked up at Lesson 8).
- **The iron law is on screen in the tester round**: slide 13 — the testing 15 minutes are untouchable; under no circumstances does the block end early or get squeezed. The tester's one-word job: **break it** — the 5-item break-it list, tried one by one, each judged **Accept or Reject**.
- **The archive ceremony closes the record**: slide 15 — five screenshots, one folder, named with name + date; missing rounds backfilled with a "backfilled" note. This session has the strictest record-completeness bar in the whole course (target ≥90%).
- **Output anchors everywhere**: slides 02 / 09 / 10 / 11 / 14 / 15 / 17 / 18 / 19 each carry a 📝 "write it in your workbook (or notebook)" action box.
- **Stand-still pages**: during hands-on time the projected page stays fixed — the screen is the students' working reference (the sentence they're about to say), not the instructor's teleprompter. Stand-still pages: 09–14 (relay rounds), 18 (buffer).
- **Zero print**: the five round openers, the lifeline, the break-it list, the Picky User phrase and the log-cleaner are NOT handed out — they live in the Student Workbook; project and read together when they debut, point students to the book when they forget.

Script/quote boxes: the one permitted fifth color — light grey `#F5F5F5` fill + 1 px black border + monospace 14–16 pt.
