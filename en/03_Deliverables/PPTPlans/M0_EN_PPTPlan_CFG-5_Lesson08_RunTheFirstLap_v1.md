## Design Principles

- **A "systems lesson" (制度课) runs on return-flips, not new pages**: the three rules (slide 05) are read aloud once in the opening, then stand still for the whole 115-minute build — the longest stand-still of the course. Slides 08/10/11 are temporary cut-ins that flip away and come straight back to 05. The institution IS the page; don't stack pages on top of it.
- **The gear change is the lesson**: Act 1→2 is the word "start" (slide 08); Act 2→3 is "hands off — review time" (slide 12). The deck's two golden-line pages — slide 02 (the shift: "From today, I only circulate. I don't teach.") and slide 17 (you ran a whole lap) — use big-type quote energy and lots of white space, set apart from the operation pages.
- **The review is the institutional floor**: slide 12's red line ("the review starts on time — nobody squeezes it, including me") and slide 15's red line ("accept everything? I'm coming to talk to you") carry the two decision-point disciplines. The review never gets cut for progress; offline it still runs — as human peer review (instructor-side, never on screen: "the review is never canceled — only its executor changes").
- **Prompts are shown whole so students can copy**: slides 05 / 13 / 14 / 16 carry full monospace boxes (6.1 / 6.2 / 6.3 / 6.4 verbatim); the BMAD entrance line (small print) rides on slide 14; slide 11 carries the two feeding lines. Everything is shared text — any edit syncs all three documents (Teacher's Guide Section 6 / this plan / the workbook).
- **The two feeding lines are the review's only food**: slide 11 tells students exactly what to prepare ("My project can ___ now / It still can't ___ yet") and why ("a bragging review is no review at all"); whoever feeds the old brief description gets sent back to rewrite. The review record (6.5) is workbook-borne — slides 13/14 done-markers point to it, no new page.
- **The appendix is optional treasure, not a fourth act**: slides 18–42 (the full Brandy case) sit after the close and are used flexibly — pick pages (suggest 19/22/26/30/34/38/42, ~10–15 min) only if time remains; a full walkthrough is ~25 min (after-school extension or next lesson's opening); no time → one line: "The appendix is in the deck — flip through it at home."
- **Output anchors everywhere**: slides 06 (the one-line goal, written at the top of the requirements sheet in large classes), 11 (two lines), 13/14 (review record), 15 (decisions), 16 (baton sheet), 17 (log line). Today's three student outputs: one running MVP, one review record, one baton handoff sheet.
- **Zero print**: the rules, the review prompts, the baton handoff sheet, and the review record are all on screen (projected) or in the workbook; the check-out register is one blank sheet per table (TA draws the columns). No physical cards go out.
- **Stand-still pages**: 05 (the three rules — 115 min, the course's longest), 06 (stand-up, ~5 min), 07 (check out, ~10 min), 13 (Picky User, ~6 min), 14 (Quinn, ~7 min), 16 (baton sheet, ~5 min). Cut-ins: 08 (start, 3 min), 09 (mid-way stand-up, 5 min), 10 (close the loop, 3 min), 11 (two lines, 2 min) — all flip back to 05. 02/03/04 are opening talk flips; 12 is the Act 3 chapter cover; 15/17 are decision/close pages.

### Colors / Type / Icons / Layout

Chaihuo brand spec (`chaihuo-ppt-brand.md`): white 70 / yellow F3D230 15 / black 10 / red D84144 5; no gradients, no shadows, no complex illustrations; 2 px black-stroke line icons; Source Han Sans; 16:9 (960×540 px); title zone 100 px top, content 110–490 px, footer 40 px. **Red appears exactly four times in this deck — slide 05's 15-minute hand-raise rule (inside the three build rules), slide 12's review-starts-on-time line, slide 13's push-back line, slide 15's accept-everything line** (four red elements, strictly capped). Warning lines that are NOT red (the no-new-windows discipline on slides 12/13/14, the feeding-lines warning on slide 11, the cross-it-out discipline on slides 05/07, the "first thing next leg" reminder on slide 15) are bold black on a yellow light fill or plain black — clearly warnings, but outside the red quota. Script/quote/template boxes: the one permitted fifth colour — light grey `#F5F5F5` fill + 1 px black border + monospace 14–16 pt; the parts students must swap in are yellow-highlighted (the `___` blanks, the real-name slot).

### On-Screen Text Rules

- All on-screen text is final English copy (below, verbatim). Digits for all numbers.
- Student-typed prompt lines are shown in monospace boxes; the part students must swap in is marked with a yellow-highlighted blank `___` (the current state / the real user / the can't-yet line).
- **On-screen display is English / numbers / graphics by design** — no platform screenshots this lesson, so the rule is naturally satisfied: every page is pure text, live hardware, or the physical Brief Wall. The cover illustration is reused from the CN deck only if it carries no Chinese glyphs; otherwise it is redrawn as a wordless Chaihuo-style version (see Asset Checklist). If garbled glyphs ever appear, tell AI *"change all on-screen text to English"* — don't debug the font library.
- During hands-on segments the page stays fixed (stand-still); instructors read from the Teacher's Guide, not from the slide.
- **Instructor-side content never appears on screen**: the two iron laws, TA A/B/C duties, the circulation rhythm (15-min laps, three looks), the triage three-liners, the crash protocol, the relative-node calls ("half of the first half done"), the backfill metrics, and the "review is never canceled — only its executor changes" line all live in the Teacher's Guide and in this plan's Instructor notes — never on the projected page.

## Backup & Downgrade

- **Live demo fails** (the opening's Brandy hook / MVP framing) → the hook is talk, not demo — just speak it; the golden line carries the point. Nothing on screen depends on a live demo.
- **Offline / AI chat down (hotspot dead)** → the build becomes "pure hardware + handwritten logic" (progress recorded in the handoff sheet); aily-blockly itself runs offline, so programming help pauses but the platform doesn't; **the review proceeds** — human peer review (the same 3-faults-5-items frame, neighbors swapped); once the network returns, a real AI review round can be made up next leg. A whole-lesson reschedule is the last resort — the three marathon legs are continuous, rescheduling costs more than downgrading (instructor-side, never on screen: the review is never canceled, only its executor changes).
- **The AI review turns into AI praise** → the push-back line is on screen (slide 13, red); project your pre-rehearsed "AI praised → I pushed back" demo conversation once.
- **Review squeezed by progress pressure** → not an option — remind table by table at "15 minutes to review" (slide 10/11); the review opens on time no matter what; one squeeze = a major backfill event (instructor-side).
- **Student device fails** → swap the device or the cable. Don't repair.
- **Project crash** (the technical path is dead) → the crash protocol: you arrive; within 15 minutes define the shrink plan ("Back to the Scope Killer: which cut lets it live?"); a leg-3 crash → the "honest failure" showcase route.
- **Fast student done in 40 minutes** → the three-rung ladder: later-list item #1 → neighbor's human tester → a one-page user manual.
- **Accept-everything** → 30-second one-on-one: "Take the 3 most important; reject the rest — rejecting is a professional decision."
- **Hot part runs out and there's an argument** → queue area on the board + the "make it or not" three questions; unused parts returned each leg; no purchase promises.
- **Time collapse** → most compressible: check out parts (10→7 min, TAs pre-sort); the mid-way stand-up (5→3 min). Next: build second half (55→45 min — close the smallest loop and release); the baton sheet (5→3 min, template copied from screen). **Never cut: the AI review 15 min + decide 10 min (the institutional floor, slides 12–15); the first stand-up; the 30-second look-back.** The appendix is the first thing to drop — it is optional by design.
- **The 15-minute rule never triggered all lesson** → don't handle live; note on the reflection page (a quality metric: students toughing it out, or goals too timid — backfill a review).
- **A student opens a new window for the review** → the discipline is on screen (slides 12/13): "Keep going in the window you have — the review has to catch your trade-off history too. A new window = a dropped baton."
- **Absent student returning (missed Lesson 7)** → the comeback flow: pick one backup topic (2 min) → TA A writes a mini requirements sheet with them in 5 min (three sections, one line each) → join the rotation normally; the review record is still written. (Missed Lesson 6 → TA-installed via the offline USB stick or pairs up with a neighbor, same as the Lesson 6 comeback.)
