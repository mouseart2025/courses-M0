## Design Principles

- **A "two-home lesson" runs on return-flips, not new pages**: each half has one soul page that stands still. Act 1's soul is slide 04 (the three checks) — read aloud once, then flipped back to during class-wide install, the import decision, and wire-it-in; the institution IS the page, don't stack pages on top of it. Act 2's soul is slide 14 (the build rules as leg one) — stand-still for the whole 40-minute build, with the quick-shell bar and the red stop line riding on it. Slides 05/08/09/10 are temporary cut-ins that flip away and come straight back to 04; slides 15/16/17 (review block) flip back to 14.
- **The gear change is the lesson**: Act 1→2 is "stand-up, go" (slide 13); Act 2→3 is "hands off — review time" (slide 15). The deck's two golden-line pages — slide 02 (the shift: "Today, your MVP grows a new power") and slide 19 (the capstone: nine lessons, and here we are) — use big-type quote energy and lots of white space, set apart from the operation pages.
- **Two teaching anchors, one per half**: slide 07 (the import decision — a trade-off decision, not a default move; today's teaching anchor, never cut) and slide 12 (the four moves — the looks upgrade, the second anchor). The lesson's institutional floor sits at slide 15's red line ("the review starts on time — nobody squeezes it out, not even me").
- **Prompts are shown whole so students can copy**: slides 04 / 08 / 09 / 15 / 16 / 17 / 18 carry full monospace boxes (6.1 / 6.2 / 6.3 / 6.4 / 6.5 / 6.8 / 6.7 verbatim — 6.6 rides as small print on slide 15); the push-back prompt rides on slide 16. Everything is shared text — any edit syncs all three documents (Teacher's Guide Section 6 / this plan / the workbook). Discipline restated (instructor-side, never on screen): both review rounds run in the same conversation — change roles without changing windows; a new window = a dropped baton.
- **Review-persona escalation, made visible on screen**: slide 15's positioning line says it plainly — "last leg's reviewer asked 'does it look right?'; THIS leg's reviewer checks 'how sturdy is it?'" — and the 5-point sturdiness checklist is tested one by one (this leg upgrades: no more picking two). The Picky User's slide 16 says "this leg, one fault." The escalation is part of the lesson, not just the teacher's plan.
- **Output anchors everywhere**: slides 06 (the three checks written into the student document), 10 (one line per student — import or why-not, heard by the room), 12 (the four moves written into the student document; a shell picked), 17 (the review record — all 5 sturdiness checks + 1 Picky fault, with results), 18 (the leg-two baton handoff sheet + the four-line log). Today's four student outputs: one library decision (or a reasoned "why not import"), one wired-in feature, a first look, one leg-two baton handoff sheet + review record.
- **Zero print**: the three checks, every prompt, the handoff sheet, and the review record are all projected (this is why slide 17 exists); the check-out record is one blank sheet per table (TA draws the columns). No physical cards go out. The library notes live in a whiteboard corner (fast-lane output, spoken or on screen, never printed).
- **Stand-still pages**: 04 (the three checks — Act 1's soul, flipped back to through install / decide / wire-in), 14 (the build rules — Act 2's soul, the whole 40-minute build), 17 (the review record — fill-in during the review), 18 (the baton handoff sheet — fill-in during decide). Cut-ins: 05 (class-wide install, 15 min), 08 (selection advisor, inside the import block), 09 (wire it in, 25 min), 10 (share & close, 5 min), 12b (materials wall, 3 min) — the install / wire-in / decide flips all return to 04. 02/03 are opening flips; 11/12 are the looks-upgrade sequence; 13 is the Act 2 opener; 15/16 flip back to 14; 19 is the close.

### Colors / Type / Icons / Layout

Chaihuo brand spec (`chaihuo-ppt-brand.md`): white 70 / yellow F3D230 15 / black 10 / red D84144 5; no gradients, no shadows, no complex illustrations; 2 px black-stroke line icons; Source Han Sans; 16:9 (960×540 px); title zone 100 px top, content 110–490 px, footer 40 px. **Red appears exactly four times in this deck — slide 07's "pick exactly one" line (the one-library rule), slide 14's "15 minutes before review, hands off everywhere" line, slide 15's "the review starts on time" line, slide 18's "accept everything? I'm coming to talk to you" line** (four red elements, strictly capped). Warning lines that are NOT red (the no-new-windows discipline on slides 15/16/17, the "AI will confidently invent libraries" line on slide 08, the function-first pull-back on slide 12, the cross-it-out discipline on slide 14, the "next leg is fix-only" reminder on slide 18) are bold black on a yellow light fill or plain black — clearly warnings, but outside the red quota. Script/quote/template boxes: the one permitted fifth colour — light grey `#F5F5F5` fill + 1 px black border + monospace 14–16 pt; the parts students must swap in are yellow-highlighted (the `___` blanks, the real-name slot).

### On-Screen Text Rules

- All on-screen text is final English copy (below, verbatim). Digits for all numbers.
- Student-typed prompt lines are shown in monospace boxes; the part students must swap in is marked with a yellow-highlighted blank `___` (the current state / the real user / the core function / the library name / the can't-yet line).
- **On-screen display is English / numbers / graphics by design** — no platform screenshots this lesson, so the rule is naturally satisfied: every page is pure text, live hardware, the physical Brief Wall, or the projected class-wide install. The cover illustration and the materials-wall illustration are reused from the CN deck only if they carry no Chinese glyphs; otherwise they are redrawn as wordless Chaihuo-style versions (see Asset Checklist). If garbled glyphs ever appear, tell AI *"change all on-screen text to English"* — don't debug the font library.
- During hands-on segments the page stays fixed (stand-still); instructors read from the Teacher's Guide, not from the slide.
- **Instructor-side content never appears on screen**: the three iron laws, the TA A/B/C duties (the triage board is today's core organ), the circulation rhythm (15-min laps, three looks), the triage three-liners, the crash protocol, the downgrade clause (no-T3 reroute / network-down reroute), the error-to-AI move, the relative-node calls ("15 minutes to review"), the backfill metrics, and the "review is never canceled — only its executor changes" line all live in the Teacher's Guide and in this plan's Instructor notes — never on the projected page.

## Backup & Downgrade (instructor-side, never on screen)

| Block | Time short (compress) | Goes wrong live (substitute) | Can't happen at all (fallback) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Class-wide install (05) | 15→12 min | hand out the offline USB | projector demo from the teacher machine + install after class |
| The import decision (07) | 15→12 min | runner-up candidate goes straight in | run the no-import route (still reviewed normally) |
| Wire it in (09) | hold "installed + example runs" | wiring moves into the build block | the bar is the acceptance |
| The looks upgrade (11–12b) | 20→12 min (keep the four moves projected) | fold into the build's last 10 minutes, teach as you go | cut it; send the four-moves image to the class group for after class |
| Build sprint (14) | 40→35 min (the quick shell can give way entirely) | lower the target, keep the core function | the crash protocol (15-minute shrink) |
| AI review (15–17) | **never cut (institutional floor)** | offline → human peer review | the desk-mate barbarian test |
| The capstone close (19) | don't cut (even 1 minute is a ritual) | — | — |

- **The downgrade clause (no T3 teacher)**: today reroutes — Act 1 becomes **a double build round + a 15-minute projector demo of the library idea** (teacher machine); the capstone close becomes a leg-two completion summary. **Never let a T1/T2 teacher gamble on the install ecosystem.**
- **Network down**: mirrors and the offline USB only cure slowness, not a dead connection — the import block moves out of today; the lesson becomes the double build round (review runs as usual); imports happen before next lesson, teacher-installed.
- **Install errors (the high-frequency segment)**: the standard move — paste the error verbatim to AI ("I tried to install this library and got this error. What does it mean? What do I do?"); AI can't solve it → teacher (version / mirror); teacher past 10 minutes → swap to the runner-up candidate.
- **Triage board (TA A)**: the whiteboard's three columns — **won't install / example won't run / logic's a mess** — sort every stuck student; TA B owns parts & hardware; TA C guards the review's no-squeeze red line and takes hand close-ups (hands only, never faces).
