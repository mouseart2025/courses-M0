## Design Principles

- **A milestone session runs two registers**: the brief arc (slides 08–16) is the whole course's attention peak — golden-line pages (08 Neil's line, 18 the ceremony) use big-type quote energy and lots of white space, set apart from the operation pages. The four-page Project Wall opening (08–11) is a quiet build-up — quote → directions → notes → the wall — that lands the re-topic lane with weight.
- **The morning half is a hands-on gauntlet, not a lecture**: slides 03–06 are stand-still operation pages; the instructor circulates only. The peer test (slide 05) is the acceptance condition — its 20 minutes are never borrowed; if time collapses, the AI-list block (slide 04) shrinks first.
- **The brief arc runs on "ask, don't answer"**: the deck never tells a student what to build or what to cut. Slide 11's re-topic self-check is projected verbatim (three questions, 5-minute timer); slide 16's veto line and slide 17's change-the-need line are the only reds outside the morning. The teacher's hand itches to pick a topic is this lesson's biggest teaching accident — instructor-side, never on screen.
- **Prompts are shown whole so students can copy**: slides 04 / 12 / 13 / 14 / 16 carry full monospace boxes (6.1 / 6.2 / 6.3 / 6.5 / 6.4 verbatim); slide 17 carries the tour task sheet (6.6 verbatim). The template on slide 14 and the task sheet on slide 17 must stay word-for-word identical to the workbook — any edit syncs all three documents (three-way sync).
- **The ceremony is the emotional peak**: slide 18 is deliberately bare — one declaration, three actions, one golden line. No code boxes, no task lists. The Fab Academy closing line (slide 19) lands the arc.
- **Output anchors everywhere**: slides 04 / 06 / 07 / 12 / 13 / 14 / 16 / 17 / 19 each carry a 📝 "write it in your workbook (or notebook)" action or a done-marker; every block's output lands in the student's own record. The requirements sheet and the tour task sheet are the two big physical outputs — both stay in the workbook, not on the wall.
- **Zero print**: the prompts, the template, and the task sheet are all on screen (projected); students write in their workbook or notebook. No physical cards go out.
- **Stand-still pages**: 03 (self-check), 04 (trouble list), 05 (peer test), 06 (fix round), 12 (PM), 13 (UX), 14 (requirements sheet), 16 (Scope Killer), 17 (tour — the longest, 34 min). Cut-in: 15 (Brandy contract, 2 min, then back to 14). Slides 08–11 are quick talk flips inside the 12-minute Project Wall beat; slide 07 is a fast log page.

### Colors / Type / Icons / Layout

Chaihuo brand spec (`chaihuo-ppt-brand.md`): white 70 / yellow F3D230 15 / black 10 / red D84144 5; no gradients, no shadows, no complex illustrations; 2 px black-stroke line icons; Source Han Sans; 16:9 (960×540 px); title zone 100 px top, content 110–490 px, footer 40 px. **Red appears exactly three times in this deck — slide 05's harassment boundary (no touching wires / no taking parts off / no covering anything except the lens), slide 16's veto line (the veto is always yours), and slide 17's change-the-need line (change the need, not the purchase)** (three red elements, strictly capped). Warning lines that are NOT red (the no-new-windows discipline on slides 12/13, the cross-it-out-don't-erase rule on slides 14/18, the facts-must-be-yours line on slide 14) are bold black on a yellow light fill or plain black — clearly warnings, but outside the red quota. Script/quote/template boxes: the one permitted fifth colour — light grey `#F5F5F5` fill + 1 px black border + monospace 14–16 pt; the parts students must swap in are yellow-highlighted (the `___` blanks, the real-name slot).

### On-Screen Text Rules

- All on-screen text is final English copy (below, verbatim). Digits for all numbers.
- Student-typed prompt lines are shown in monospace boxes; the part students must swap in is marked with a yellow-highlighted blank `___` (the threshold / the real name / the pasted sheet).
- **On-screen display is English / numbers / graphics by design** — this lesson has no platform screenshots, so the rule is naturally satisfied: every page is pure text, live hardware, or the physical walls (Project Wall, Brief Wall). If garbled glyphs ever appear, tell AI *"change all on-screen text to English"* — don't debug the font library.
- During hands-on segments the page stays fixed (stand-still); instructors read from the Teacher's Guide, not from the slide.
- **Instructor-side content never appears on screen**: the iron law, the peer-test-is-untouchable rule, TA duties, the backup topics, and all pitfalls live in the Teacher's Guide and in this plan's Instructor notes — never on the projected page.
