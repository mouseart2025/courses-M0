## Design Principles

- **A "two-peak lesson" gives its peaks room**: slide 11 (the roadshow) is the course's fewest-words page — three rules in oversized type, nothing else to read; slide 13 (the ceremony) is the whole series' ritual peak — big type, wide white space, the ten-lesson journey growing toward "TODAY — you told its story," no code boxes, no task lists. The two peaks get their full minutes; everything else compresses before they do.
- **Each act's soul page stands still**: slide 04 (the sprint — two jobs only + fix-only) holds for the whole 45-minute sprint; slide 11 (the three roadshow rules) holds for the whole 32-minute roadshow. Slides 05/06 flip back to 04; Act 2's four pages (07–10) are a forward sequence — each cut-in flips forward into the next block, not back; slide 13 stays projected from the ceremony's start to the photo, no more flips.
- **The gear change is the lesson**: Act 1→2 is "hands off — what passes the release check earns the right to be told" (slide 05); Act 2→3 is "pitch closed — real humans on stage" (slide 11). The deck's two golden-line moments — slide 02 (the final-day map: "Today has exactly one job — make your project understood") and slide 13 (the bookend: "You're not watching the magic anymore — you're making it") — use big-type quote energy, set apart from the operation pages.
- **The two hard gates are the lesson's master switch** (instructor-side, never on screen): the sprint stops when Act 1 ends — slide 04's red line points to the gate; the ceremony begins 15 minutes before the end — slide 13's red line is the gate itself. Roadshow remainder after the gate: present after the ceremony or post the video to the class group.
- **Prompts and templates are shown whole so students can copy**: slides 05 / 07 (×2: prompt + copy-out template) / 08 / 09 / 10 carry full monospace boxes (6.1 / 6.2 / 6.3 / 6.4 / 6.5 verbatim); the push-back prompt rides on slide 10; slides 11 (three rules + self-check) and 13 (journey + last log + photo rule) carry the ceremony's projected blocks (6.6 / 6.7 / 6.8). Everything is shared text — any edit syncs all three documents (Teacher's Guide Section 6 / this plan / the workbook). Discipline restated (instructor-side, never on screen): every AI round runs in the original project conversation — it knows the context of all ten logs.
- **The three hard references give the pitch bones**: slide 09 marks them with yellow dots — "who it's for" must be a real person from the Lesson-2 list; "what it's worth" must quote the account from slide 08; "the problem" must be the requirements sheet's own words. All three real, and the page has bones — the pitch's anti-slogan control.
- **Output anchors everywhere**: slides 03 (one fix-only goal per student), 05 (a release checklist with every item concluded + a confirmed "how do I save it"), 07 (a proofread README), 08 (one number + one reason), 09 (a four-box pitch with all three hard references verified), 10 (a pitch that survived one investor round), 11 (every student roadshows at desk level — 100% is the acceptance line), 13 (the last log + the photo). Today's four student outputs: one README, one two-line account, one one-page pitch, one 2-minute roadshow.
- **Zero print + recording rules**: README template, pitch four boxes, roadshow self-check, "fix-only," the parent line — all projected; students copy into notebooks / student documents. Recording rules (hands only, never faces) ride on slide 13's red line and live in every instructor note — the guardian-consent list decides who may be on camera.
- **Stand-still pages**: 04 (the sprint — 45 min, Act 1's soul), 11 (the roadshow — 32 min, Act 3's soul), 13 (the ceremony — projected from start to photo, no more flips). Cut-ins: 05 (release check, 15 min), 06 (engineer's checklist, inside the release block), 07 (README, 20 min), 08 (math, 12 min), 09 (pitch, 15 min), 10 (AI investor, 10 min). 02 is the opening flip; 03 is the stand-up; 12 is the vote (flip in, flip out to 13).

### On-Screen Text Rules

- All on-screen text is final English copy (below, verbatim). Digits for all numbers.
- Student-typed prompt lines are shown in monospace boxes; the part students must swap in is marked with a yellow-highlighted blank `___` (the current state / the can't-yet line / the price / the reason / the real person's name).
- **On-screen display is English / numbers / graphics by design** — no platform screenshots this lesson, so the rule is naturally satisfied: every page is pure text, vector graphics, live hardware, or the physical Brief Wall. The cover illustration is reused from the CN deck only if it carries no Chinese glyphs; otherwise it is redrawn as a wordless Chaihuo-style version (see Asset Checklist). If garbled glyphs ever appear, tell AI *"change all on-screen text to English"* — don't debug the font library.
- During hands-on segments the page stays fixed (stand-still); instructors read from the Teacher's Guide, not from the slide.
- **Instructor-side content never appears on screen**: the three iron laws, the two hard gates, the TA A/B/C duties, the circulation rhythm (15-min laps, three looks), the parent-audience flow and consent list, the offline downgrade, the time elastic band, the backfill metrics, the honest-failure line, and the partner/TTT-scene swap (the value round + the time-and-people column) all live in the Teacher's Guide and in this plan's Instructor notes — never on the projected page.

**Instructor notes** (verbatim) *(the lite line — the red line goes up first)*: *"Rule first: today we count exactly two things — what it cost, and what it's worth. No profits, no business talk. Step two: if you sold it, what would you price it at? And why? Write one number, write one reason. Note: the price can be above cost — even well above — but the reason must be 'it solves this problem,' not 'I want it.'"* The two-line account is the middle-school red line — the "no profits, no business" line is spoken before anything else, and the locked parent line stays instructor-side ("We're teaching how to explain a creation — cost is applied math, value is expression training. We're not teaching business."). Pitfall stays instructor-side: "Ten thousand!" → catch it ("Fine — now make the 'why' hold up. Can't? Lower the price until you can.") — the core move of value-expression training, never a scold.
